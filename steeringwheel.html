Project Overview

Redesign of the Baja SAE steering wheel focused on ergonomics, manufacturability, and durability. Targets: reduce mass, improve driver comfort and hand placement, and maintain a robust safety factor under worst-case event loads.

Project Goals

• Improve driver ergonomics and visibility of key controls
• Reduce mass while preserving stiffness and strength
• Ensure compatibility with existing steering column & quick-release
• Design for waterjet/CNC routing and simple post-processing

Context & Constraints

The steering wheel interfaces with our 2025 steering column and quick-release hub. It must withstand operational loads (cornering, bumps) and extreme scenarios (sudden impacts), while remaining comfortable for drivers with different hand sizes and gloves.

  • Competition: Baja SAE (2025 ruleset)
  • Material candidates: 6061-T6 aluminum, carbon sandwich, 3D-printed nylon w/ CF
  • Manufacturing: waterjet/CNC, chamfers/radiusing for grip comfort

Design Process

Started with sketches and ergonomic mockups to decide on outer profile and thumb placements. Iterated in CAD (SolidWorks) with parametric dimensions for rapid changes. Fasteners and hub pattern modeled to ensure clearances. Fillets applied to reduce stress concentrations.

FEA Setup & Results

Boundary conditions: hub bolt circle constrained; applied hand torque and vertical pull to simulate harsh maneuvering and impact. Mesh refined around spoke fillets and hub interface.

  • Max von Mises stress: < 40% of 6061-T6 yield (safety factor > 2.5)
  • Predominant stress risers at spoke–hub fillets → increased radius + local thickening
  • Deflection at rim under 100 N pull: < 1.5 mm

Ergonomics & Controls

Rim diameter and grip thickness tuned from driver feedback. Thumb rests positioned to minimize wrist strain. Optional switch plate for kill-switch and display buttons with guarded cutouts to prevent accidental activation.

Manufacturing Plan

  1. Waterjet/CNC route 6061-T6 plate (6–8 mm)
  2. Deburr, tumble, and hand-finish grip edges
  3. Optional pocketing on rear face for mass reduction
  4. Anodize; apply grip tape at thumb rests
  5. Assembly with hub and switch plate

Outcomes & Next Steps

Prototype met stiffness and comfort targets in bench tests. Next: on-vehicle testing, fatigue assessment, and exploring a carbon-sandwich variant with 3D-printed core for further weight savings without compromising stiffness.
